### 2.1 Local Government Financial Accountability and Audit

**Commission on Audit (COA). (2022). *Annual Audit Reports on Local Government Units.***

COA annual audit reports document recurring LGU compliance issues, including delayed submissions and incomplete documentation. These findings justify systems that improve traceability and timeliness of supporting paperwork—directly aligned with SmartFlow’s goal of visible document movement and COA-aligned flow summaries.

**Department of Budget and Management (DBM). (2022). *Enhanced Public Financial Management Assessment Tool (ePFMAT) for Local Government Units.***

The ePFMAT framework evaluates LGU financial management practices and surfaces weaknesses in document flow and reporting. SmartFlow responds by QR-based tracking and automated generation of document flow metrics (processing time, late counts, completion rates) that support—not replace—Accounting’s COA preparation.

### 2.2 Digital Transformation and Governance Reform

**Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS). (2022). *Digital Transformation in Local Government Units.***

PIDS policy research links digital tools in LGUs to efficiency, transparency, and citizen trust. SmartFlow fits this direction by adding **QR-based tracking** to existing paper workflows rather than requiring full digitization—appropriate for municipalities with limited IT maturity (as in Urbiztondo, where no MIS is currently in use).

**Asian Development Bank (ADB). (2022). *Philippines: Local Governance Reform Project.***

ADB work on local governance reform highlights operational inefficiencies and recommends practical digital interventions. SmartFlow’s inter-department monitoring is consistent with reform goals: measurable flow, reduced bureaucratic delay.

**World Bank. (2022). *Philippines Local Governance Reform Program.***

World Bank emphasis on accountability and transparency supports making government processes visible and measurable. SmartFlow’s dashboard and department analytics turn informal follow-ups into logged, reportable evidence.

### 2.3 Records Management in LGUs

**Tabia, V. U. (2022). *Records Management and Data Security in Local Government Units.* Provincial Government of Laguna.**

Tabia stresses proper records management and secure handling in LGUs. SmartFlow contributes efficiency and accountability through scan-based custody logs while remaining within scope by tracking metadata and movement—not storing full document images in v1.

**Balangat, E. S. (2022). Status of the Records Management System of LGU Calanasan: Recommendation for Records Management System. *ISRG Journal of Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences.***

Balangat’s evaluation of another LGU’s records practices reports delayed submissions, poor accessibility, and lack of transparency—the same pain points identified in Urbiztondo’s Accounting interview. SmartFlow targets those gaps for the four pilot offices and three financial document types.

### 2.5 Synthesis

National audit and reform literature, LGU records studies, and Urbiztondo’s manual process (calls, visits, group chats, Excel consolidation) converge on one need: **visibility and measurable flow** of financial documents across departments. Existing LGU systems often push full digitization; SmartFlow’s proposal is distinct—it **tracks physical documents** via QR, flags delays, and feeds COA-oriented summaries without generating financial statements. No reviewed study yet combines this exact pilot scope (Accounting, Budget, HR, Engineering; DVs, payroll, budgets) for Urbiztondo.
