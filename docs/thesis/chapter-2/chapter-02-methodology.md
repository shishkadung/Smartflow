## 2.1 Design of Software, System, Product or Process

This section presents the **design** of SmartFlow as a hybrid document flow tracking system for the Municipality of Urbiztondo. The design covers software architecture, data flows, development life cycle, operational flowchart, database structure, and user interactions. Diagram labels and flows for Word or Lucidchart are provided in [design-diagrams-for-word.md](design-diagrams-for-word.md).

SmartFlow tracks **physical** financial documents—disbursement vouchers, payroll records, and approved budgets—among **Engineering, HR, Budget, and Accounting** using **QR codes**, without storing the content of those documents in version 1.

---

### 2.1.1 Software Architecture

SmartFlow uses a **three-tier client–server architecture** with two presentation clients and one shared backend.

The **presentation layer** consists of (1) a **Flutter** mobile application on **Android** smartphones for QR **scan-in** and **scan-out** at each office handoff, and (2) a **React** web portal on desktop or laptop browsers for document registration, QR printing, real-time dashboard, delay alerts, COA-aligned flow reports, and department analytics.

The **application layer** is a **PHP REST API** hosted on an **Apache** server at the LGU. It handles authentication, role-based access, document metadata, scan logging, threshold comparison, alert generation, and report aggregation. Both Flutter and React send and receive **JSON** over **HTTPS** on the municipal **LAN or Wi-Fi**.

The **data layer** is a **MySQL** relational database storing users, roles, offices, documents, scan logs, processing thresholds, and alerts.

This architecture supports the seven specific objectives in Chapter I: mobile scanning (Objective 2), web registration and QR tagging (Objective 1), dashboard and alerts (Objectives 3–4), and reports and analytics (Objectives 5–6), with evaluation through pilot use (Objective 7).

*See **Figure 2-1. Software Architecture of the SmartFlow System** (`design-diagrams-for-word.md`).*

### 2.1.3 Software Development Life Cycle

The researchers adopt an **Iterative Software Development Life Cycle** (Agile-style prototyping), suited to limited LGU IT capacity and the need to validate a simple scan workflow early.

| Phase | Activity | SmartFlow output |
|-------|----------|------------------|
| **Planning** | Scope, MOA, timeline | Project plan aligned with four offices and three document types |
| **Analysis** | Interview, observation | Requirements; as-is and to-be process (§2.2) |
| **Design** | Architecture, DFD, ERD, UI | Figures 2-1 to 2-7; `docs/srs.md` |
| **Implementation** | Coding by objective | Flutter, React, PHP/MySQL modules |
| **Testing** | Unit, integration, UAT | Test logs; defect fixes |
| **Deployment** | Pilot on LGU server/phones | Live SmartFlow at Urbiztondo |
| **Evaluation** | ISO/IEC 25010 questionnaire | Results for Objective 7 |
| **Maintenance** | Handover | User guide; LGU support |

Development proceeds in **seven increments**, each mapped to one specific objective (QR tagging → scan-and-forward → dashboard → alerts → COA reports → analytics → evaluation). After each increment, a prototype is demonstrated for feedback before the next sprint.

*See **Figure 2-4. Software Development Life Cycle of SmartFlow** (`design-diagrams-for-word.md`).*

### 2.1.4 System Flowchart

SmartFlow is described using **three role-based system flowcharts** (Figures 2-5, 2-5b, and 2-5c). The study clarifies **who does what** before mapping specific office-to-office routes for each document type. Standard flowchart symbols are used: ovals for start and end, rectangles for processes, and diamonds for decisions.

**Figure 2-5 — Department member (clerk).** The clerk logs in to the **Flutter** mobile application. When a physical document arrives at the office, the clerk scans the QR code and selects **receive**. When the document is sent to another office, the clerk scans and selects **forward**. The system saves the audit trail and updates the dashboard. This flow is the **same for any frontline staff** who handle documents, regardless of office. Clerks do not register new documents or print QR labels in the primary workflow.

**Figure 2-5b — Department head.** The head logs in to the **React** web portal and opens the **office dashboard** for that office only. The head views documents currently in the office, processing time, and overdue flags. If a delay alert appears, the head follows up with staff. The head may view office-level analytics. Scanning is **not** required unless the head also performs clerk duties.

**Figure 2-5c — Municipal Accountant / Accounting staff.** The Accountant logs in to the **React** web portal and opens a **municipal-wide dashboard** showing active documents, locations, processing times, and overdue items across all pilot offices—not one office only. The Accountant may **register** a new financial document and **generate or print a QR label** when registration occurs at Accounting or as authorized encoder. The Accountant **monitors** inter-department flow and **delay alerts**, and verifies that **receive scans** were recorded when documents arrive; if a scan was skipped, follow-up with the originating office is manual. **Financial consolidation** and **financial statements** remain outside SmartFlow (manual Excel work). The Accountant generates **COA-aligned flow summary reports** (monthly, quarterly, annual) and **department performance analytics**, **exports** PDF or Excel files for COA support, and marks documents **completed** when tracking ends.

If any handoff **scan is skipped**, the audit trail is incomplete, as stated in Chapter I limitations.

**Figure 2-5d — System administrator.** The administrator logs in to the **React** web portal and opens the **administration** module. The administrator **creates and maintains user accounts**, assigns **roles** (department member, department head, municipal accountant, administrator) and **office** assignments, configures **pilot offices**, and sets **processing time thresholds** used for overdue alerts. The administrator may review optional **system logs** or maintenance tasks. The administrator does **not** perform daily QR scanning, generate COA flow reports, or act as the Municipal Accountant in document monitoring. Server hosting remains an LGU responsibility.
